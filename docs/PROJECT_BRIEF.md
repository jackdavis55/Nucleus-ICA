# NUCLEUS — Project Brief

## 1. Product vision

NUCLEUS is a structured Just Culture assessment tool for safety-critical organisations, initially focused on nuclear power generation. It guides a qualified assessor through an evidence-informed assessment of an individual's actions and the circumstances surrounding a significant loss incident, unsafe activity, non-conformance or abnormal event.

The application should help the assessor understand:
- What the individual did or did not do.
- What they intended and understood at the time.
- Whether procedures and expectations were clear, accurate and workable.
- What human-performance error traps and mitigating factors were present.
- How much meaningful choice and control the individual had.
- How the organisation, supervision, work design and safety barriers contributed.
- What human performance classification best describes the behaviour.
- What proportionate learning, corrective or accountability response may be appropriate.

## 2. Product positioning

The product is a decision-support and structured reasoning environment, not:
- An automated culpability calculator.
- A legal determination of negligence or recklessness.
- A substitute for a competent investigation.
- A disciplinary decision-maker.
- A replacement for site procedures, employment policies or regulatory requirements.

The assessor remains responsible for evaluating evidence and conclusions.

## 3. User and operating model

- Primary user: one individual assessor (e.g. manager, HR professional, investigation lead or human factors specialist).
- Standalone use: one assessor works through one assessment at a time.
- Each assessment concerns one individual in relation to one incident/event or defined unsafe act.
- The MVP does not require collaboration, user accounts, multi-tenant support or enterprise workflows.
- The application should be usable on a desktop/laptop and adapt to smaller screens.

## 4. Product principles

1. Context before judgement.
2. Evidence before conclusion.
3. Distinguish action, intention, understanding, volition and outcome.
4. Examine organisational conditions as well as individual behaviour.
5. Do not equate deliberate action with reckless behaviour.
6. Do not equate procedural deviation with knowing violation.
7. Treat unknown and disputed information honestly.
8. Keep Reason's human error taxonomy separate from Just Culture accountability categories.
9. Avoid numerical culpability scores.
10. Make the reasoning transparent, reviewable and editable.
11. Use neutral questions and avoid double negatives.
12. Protect sensitive personal and operational information.

## 5. Core user journey

1. Create an assessment.
2. Enter a fictional or appropriately authorised incident summary.
3. Identify the individual and the specific action/omission being assessed.
4. Work through the guided assessment stages.
5. Record answers, why explanations, evidence and error traps.
6. Review the evolving narrative and outstanding information.
7. Review Reason-style human performance classifications.
8. Review volition and organisational/systemic analysis.
9. Consider recommendations.
10. Record a provisional overall assessment and rationale.
11. Export or print a report.

## 6. Design direction

- Modern, restrained enterprise interface suitable for nuclear/high-hazard organisations.
- Deep navy, industrial teal, neutral grey, restrained amber and muted red.
- Clear left sidebar showing all assessment stages and status.
- Main workspace containing one focused question panel at a time.
- Persistent or readily accessible current assessment narrative.
- Evidence and error-factor panels should be expandable, not overwhelm the primary question.
- Strong readability, accessibility and clear status labels.

## 7. MVP acceptance outcome

A user should be able to complete a fictional demonstration assessment from start to finish, see the narrative update as answers are entered, review classifications and recommendations, and produce a coherent draft report. The application must visibly label conclusions as provisional and explain missing or disputed evidence.
