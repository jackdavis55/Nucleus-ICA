# NUCLEUS — Literature and Reference Register

This register is a research checklist. Verify the exact edition, publication details, figure numbers, page references and terminology against copies of the source material before embedding them in product guidance or claiming that a particular question reproduces an author's model.

## Core sources to verify

### James Reason
- Reason, James (1997). *Managing the Risks of Organizational Accidents*. Ashgate.
- Review the discussion of human error, unsafe acts, violations, organisational accidents and the just-culture/culpability decision tree.
- Verify the exact figure and page references in the edition being used.
- Capture the original terminology accurately. Do not silently combine distinct Reason models.

### Sidney Dekker
- Dekker, Sidney. *Just Culture: Balancing Safety and Accountability* (verify edition/year used).
- Dekker, Sidney (2008). *Just Culture: Who Gets to Draw the Line?* (verify publication details and source).
- Review the critique of decision-tree approaches, the social/organisational nature of culpability judgements, and the treatment of volition/personal control.
- Verify exact terms and conceptual structure before attributing a named model to Dekker.

### International Atomic Energy Agency
- IAEA Safety Standards Series SSG-76, *Conduct of Operations at Nuclear Power Plants*.
- Review relevant human-performance, procedure-use, communication, briefing, checking and operational-conduct guidance.
- Verify publication metadata and exact paragraph references.

### U.S. Department of Energy
- Human Performance Improvement Handbook / relevant DOE human performance guidance.
- Verify the exact handbook title, revision, date and sections before citation.

### UK Office for Nuclear Regulation
- Relevant ONR safety culture guidance and Nuclear Industry Safety Culture Inventory material.
- Verify current version, date, applicability and exact language before relying on it.

## Evidence register fields for future use

For every source used in the application, record:
- Full bibliographic citation.
- Edition/revision and publication date.
- Page, figure, paragraph or section.
- Exact concept being used.
- Application question or design feature informed by it.
- Whether the application paraphrases, adapts or directly quotes it.
- Verification status.
- Reviewer and review date.

## Copyright and attribution

Prefer concise paraphrases and proper citations. Do not copy substantial copyrighted decision-tree text or reproduce figures without confirming permission/licensing. The application should present its own clearly attributed implementation, not imply endorsement by the authors or organisations.
